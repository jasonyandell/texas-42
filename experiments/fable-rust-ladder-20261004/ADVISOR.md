## Assessment of the Rust ladder results (advisor, read-only)

I read REPORT.md, STATUS.md, main.rs, net.rs, train.py, h2h.py, every results/h2h-*/summary.json, the model JSONs, and the two design docs. Verdict first: the engineering is sound (the harness control at 0 flips, the same outer seed for both players, resumable workers). The interpretation is ahead of the data in three places, the fidelity metric you report is the wrong one for this seam, and the single experiment that settles "can this rung collapse" has not been run yet.

### Conclusions not yet supported

1. **"The inner level is not where Walt's strength lives."** The ascending control says the native inner is worth 8.6 paired points over no model at all; the best net recovers about 83% of that, not all of it. The 64-world control at 96 deals (+.031, CI ±.08) supports nothing in either direction. Also, the effective sample is the ~1,000 flipped deals per arm, not 4,096: in every arm roughly 26% of deals flip and the rest are insensitive, so 8.6 points is a large share of the deals that can move.
2. **"Two-layer nets nearly reach parity."** −.015 [−.030, .000] and −.014 [−.029, +.001] are losses at the 95% edge, across more than ten arms with no multiplicity correction. Call it "within 1.5 points", not parity.
3. **The one-layer-256 number.** Same weight file (`full-h256x1.w`, written 14:57, unmodified) gave −.009 [−.040, +.022] on seed 4000 and −.061 [−.076, −.045] on seed 5000. Those CIs do not overlap; that is a ~3 SE discrepancy for a deterministic harness on i.i.d. fresh deals. Either the bootstrap understates variance or something differed between runs. Pool it (~−.05 over 5,120 deals) and say the one-layer picture is uncertain, not "4,096 supersedes 1,024".

A number you have and did not report: `val_agree_hires` in the model JSONs is .486 (h32), .535 (h256x1), .577 (h256x2), while the production 8-world inner agrees with the 160-world argmax 60% of the time. Every net is a *worse* predictor of the teacher's own argmax than eight random worlds, yet the deep ones are near parity in h2h. That is only consistent if most disagreements are near-ties that do not matter, which means RMSE on the vector and raw agreement are both the wrong gauge for this seam.

### Ranked recommendations

**1. Run the exact inner at 16 and 32 worlds, properly powered (4,096 deals).** This is the experiment that settles the main question and it is cheap: the 64-world control cost 324 ms/decision only because `HiRes` rebuilds `Shared`/`Solver` and BigRationals per call; a 32-world inner is ~4x the native call, so ~15 ms/decision, about one 295 s round on 12 workers. It gives the ceiling for "deterministic argmax-of-expectation inner" with zero approximation error. If hires32 also sits at −.01 to −.02, the net has reached the ceiling of this seam, the residual is the noise-structure effect (concern 3), and more capacity is wasted. If hires32 is at +.02, the gap is approximation or distribution shift and is closable. Nothing else you can run discriminates these two worlds.

**2. Replace h2h as the screening statistic with decision-level paired regret under a shared reference bundle.** You already have 95% of it: `bench` replays native roots and asks the hybrid at the same public node with the same `seed`, so both outers see identical 40 worlds; the hybrid's vector is in `v["evaluation"]["options"]`. Add a reference vector per root computed once (K=4 to 8 seeds of n=40 native, per DESIGN §4, not n=320) in a shared fixture, then score each arm by `V_ref[choice_native] − V_ref[choice_arm]` in role sign. The reference's sampling error is paired across arms and averages out over 10k roots; the SE is two orders of magnitude below h2h's. "The right move loses" dissolves here: the player's criterion is expected make under its belief, and regret measures exactly that; the realized deal is the noise, which is why h2h needs 4,096 deals to see 1.5 points. Keep h2h as the final confirmation for one or two arms, and log roots from hybrid self-play too so compounding off-distribution error is seen.

**3. Ten times the deal coverage at the same label count.** 2.17M calls from 48 deals is a lot of nodes from very few public histories; validation is 8 games. Native self-play logging costs ~0.15 s per game, so 500 games is nothing; use the existing `--cap` to subsample calls per decision. The earlier T0 study found coverage was a top-two factor. This is the cheapest likely win for every arm.

**4. Distill the stochastic policy, or at least test it.** The native inner is a random function of its seed with 60% top-choice stability; the net replaces it with a deterministic argmax. Inside the outer, the hidden seats become perfectly predictable and the committed-own-future recursion best-responds to a brittle model. Two cheap tests: (a) seeded Gumbel noise on the net's scores, calibrated so agreement with the 160-world argmax is ~60%, deterministic per call hash; (b) relabel at 160 worlds keeping per-world outcomes, derive the 8-world argmax distribution from the 20 disjoint sub-bundles, and train a choice-distribution head sampled with the call hash. If (a) or (b) closes the last 1.5 points, concern 3 is confirmed and the next rung's teacher must be "expected vector, sampled choice" throughout.

**5. Loss aligned to the consumer.** The outer consumes a tile. Masked BCE spends capacity calibrating absolute values the outer never reads. For inner rungs train a ranking objective (pairwise hinge or listwise CE on the 160-world counts with a temperature), select checkpoints on regret-at-resolved-gaps, and report that curve. Only the top rung needs a calibrated vector for the display.

**6. Architecture: NNUE proper, not deeper dense.** The v2 gain (2 points from indexing tile rows by declaration, same depth) is direct evidence the one-layer nets fail on conjunctions (trump × tile × led suit × table), not on width. Two options: a wide sparse first layer (512 to 1024) kept incrementally across the recursion (parent-to-child changes 1 to 5 tile states, so the update is ~5 rows instead of 28) feeding two tiny layers (32, 32) in int8; or a per-legal-tile scorer over ~30 rule features (wins trick, trump, count points, master in suit, unseen followers, partner winning, position, can be overtrumped) with a shared 32→32→1 MLP, which is about 5k MACs per call. Either is sub-microsecond; the second is also the most sample-efficient per your earlier study. Also batch all alive worlds at a public node into one matrix multiply; that alone is 2 to 4x on NEON.

### On the seam and the next rung (concern 1)

The cut is right: the outer consumes a tile, so distill a policy there and nothing more. Do not distill the outer's per-world continuation value; that replaces the recursion, which is rung 2. For linear compute: net_0 ≈ pi(0); the teacher for net_1 is outer(net_0)'s choice at logged information states (K-seed average at n=40, so "expectation of the deployed estimator"), and outer(net_1) is the level-2 player. Each rung is one logging run, one label run, one train, one regret screen, one h2h against the rung below. The logging must cover all four seats' information states, which self-play with the net inside provides at ~1 ms per decision.

### Smallest path to the answer

Run 1 and 2 this session. If hires32 is at parity and the two-layer net's regret-on-resolved-gaps is near zero, the rung collapses and the remaining work is 6 (speed). If hires32 is above native, run 3 and 4 before touching architecture.
