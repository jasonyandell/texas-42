# 1. Order exchange: the repair of parent §7.1

## 1.1 Why the parent statement is unsound

Parent §7.1 asserts: if two lawful focal action blocks \(a,b\) satisfy
\(\mathsf K_a\star\mathsf K_b=\mathsf K_b\star\mathsf K_a\) as joint kernels of
(public observation trace, accumulated outcome, successor information
interface), then *every continuation that differs only by exchanging the order
of \(a\) and \(b\) has the same policy-indexed terminal law*.

Two independent defects.

**(D1) Under a literal reading the hypothesis is unsatisfiable.** The public
trace records each play together with the seat that made it. Executing block
\(a\) and then block \(b\) produces traces whose focal component is \(a\) then
\(b\); executing \(b\) then \(a\) produces traces whose focal component is \(b\)
then \(a\). For \(a\ne b\) these are disjoint sets of traces, so

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf K_a\star\mathsf K_b)
\cap
\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf K_b\star\mathsf K_a)
=\varnothing ,
\]

and the two kernels are never equal. Read literally, the theorem is vacuous:
its hypothesis holds for no pair of distinct blocks in this game.

**(D2) Under the intended "up to declared transports" reading the proof step is
invalid.** The intended hypothesis is that the two sides agree after a bijection
\(\Theta\) exchanging the two blocks' labels. The parent's proof then says:
"compose both sides with the same lawful successor policy kernel." Under
treatment \(H\) the focal seat has perfect recall, so a lawful continuation
policy is a function of the trace. A policy \(\pi\) applied after order
\((a,b)\) is being applied to the traces \(\tau\); applied after order
\((b,a)\) it meets the traces \(\Theta\tau\). "The same policy" therefore
denotes \(\pi\) on one side and \(\pi\circ\Theta\) on the other, and equality of
the composed laws requires \(\pi=\pi\circ\Theta\) — a restriction on the policy,
not a consequence of the kernel identity. Concretely: nothing prevents a lawful
policy from behaving differently at its next decision according to which of the
two tiles it played first, and such a policy generally yields different terminal
laws in the two orders even when the kernels intertwine perfectly.

The repair keeps the mathematics and moves the conclusion to the object that is
invariant: the **set** of achievable terminal laws, hence the optimal
continuation value.

## 1.2 Setting

Fix a finite-horizon fixed-field process as in the parent §2: decision state
\(B=(K,e,\beta)\), focal seat \(m\), declared field \(\sigma_{-m}\), outcome
monoid \(\mathcal M\) of the declared contract, and utility \(U\) readable from
the preserved outcome contract.

An **action block** is a finite prescription of focal behaviour covering a
contiguous segment of play, from a node at which the focal seat is to act until
a declared resumption boundary, together with the field and chance responses
inside that segment. For a block \(a\), let

\[
\mathsf K_a(\xi;\tau,m,\xi')
\]

be the joint law, from latent state \(\xi\), of the public trace segment
\(\tau\), the accumulated outcome \(m\in\mathcal M\), and the successor latent
state \(\xi'\). Composition \(\star\) is kernel composition with outcome
multiplication in \(\mathcal M\).

**(H0) Observation-determined typing.** The successor information interface is a
function of the trace. (This is the standing requirement of the interface
machinery; it is what makes a policy "a function of the trace" well typed, and
it is satisfied by the public-trick observation contract.)

Write \(T_{ab}\) and \(T_{ba}\) for the sets of two-block traces produced by the
orders \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\). For a continuation policy \(\pi\) — lawful from
the resumption boundary, hence a function of the trace — write
\(\Lambda_{\pi(\tau)}(\xi')\in\operatorname{Dist}(\mathcal M)\) for the terminal
outcome law from \(\xi'\) under the branch \(\pi\) selects after \(\tau\).

## 1.3 Theorem E1 (order exchange under a declared involution)

*Repairs parent §7.1; authorised by DS-A6.*

**Hypotheses.**

- **(H1)** \(\Theta:T_{ab}\to T_{ba}\) is a bijection exchanging the two blocks'
  labels. (Extending \(\Theta\) to the disjoint union
  \(T_{ab}\sqcup T_{ba}\) by its inverse makes it an involution; that form is
  preferred and is why the theorem is named for one.)
- **(H2) Intertwining.** For every \(\xi\),

  \[
  \boxed{
  \mathsf K_b\star\mathsf K_a
  =
  \Theta_*\bigl(\mathsf K_a\star\mathsf K_b\bigr)
  }
  \]

  as kernels \(X\to\operatorname{Dist}(T\times\mathcal M\times X)\); explicitly,
  \((\mathsf K_b\star\mathsf K_a)(\xi;\Theta\tau,m,\xi')
  =(\mathsf K_a\star\mathsf K_b)(\xi;\tau,m,\xi')\) for all
  \(\tau\in T_{ab}\), \(m\in\mathcal M\), \(\xi'\in X\).
- **(H3) \(\Theta\)-closure of the policy class.** For every lawful continuation
  policy \(\pi\), the policy \(\pi\circ\Theta\) — which behaves at trace
  \(\Theta\tau\) as \(\pi\) behaves at \(\tau\) — is again lawful; and likewise
  for \(\pi\circ\Theta^{-1}\).

**Conclusion.** For every \(\xi\in X\), the set of terminal outcome laws
achievable from \(\xi\) by "block \(a\), then block \(b\), then a lawful
continuation" equals the set achievable by "block \(b\), then block \(a\), then
a lawful continuation":

\[
\boxed{
\{\,\mathcal L(ab,\pi)(\xi):\pi\text{ lawful}\,\}
=
\{\,\mathcal L(ba,\pi)(\xi):\pi\text{ lawful}\,\}.
}
\]

Consequently, for every utility readable from the preserved outcome contract and
every belief \(\beta\), the two orders have the same **optimal** continuation
value, and the same optimal value conditional on any event measurable with
respect to \(\Theta\)-invariant data.

**Proof.** Fix \(\xi\) and a lawful continuation \(\pi\). Writing
\(\mu_\xi=(\mathsf K_a\star\mathsf K_b)(\xi;\cdot)\) and
\(\nu_\xi=(\mathsf K_b\star\mathsf K_a)(\xi;\cdot)\), the terminal law under
order \((a,b)\) with continuation \(\pi\) is

\[
\mathcal L(ab,\pi)(\xi)
=
\sum_{\tau\in T_{ab}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\sum_{\xi'\in X}
\mu_\xi(\tau,m,\xi')\;
m\cdot\Lambda_{\pi(\tau)}(\xi'),
\]

where \(m\cdot(-)\) is translation of the law by \(m\) in \(\mathcal M\).
Similarly, for a lawful \(\pi'\),

\[
\mathcal L(ba,\pi')(\xi)
=
\sum_{\tau'\in T_{ba}}\sum_{m}\sum_{\xi'}
\nu_\xi(\tau',m,\xi')\;
m\cdot\Lambda_{\pi'(\tau')}(\xi').
\]

Substituting \(\tau'=\Theta\tau\) and applying (H2),

\[
\mathcal L(ba,\pi')(\xi)
=
\sum_{\tau\in T_{ab}}\sum_{m}\sum_{\xi'}
\mu_\xi(\tau,m,\xi')\;
m\cdot\Lambda_{\pi'(\Theta\tau)}(\xi').
\]

Choose \(\pi':=\pi\circ\Theta^{-1}\), so that \(\pi'(\Theta\tau)=\pi(\tau)\) for
every \(\tau\in T_{ab}\); by (H3) \(\pi'\) is lawful. The two displayed sums are
then term-by-term identical, so
\(\mathcal L(ba,\pi')(\xi)=\mathcal L(ab,\pi)(\xi)\). The map
\(\pi\mapsto\pi\circ\Theta^{-1}\) is injective on continuation branches and has
inverse \(\pi'\mapsto\pi'\circ\Theta\), which is lawful by (H3); so the two
achievable sets are in bijection and coincide. Equality of sets gives equality of
suprema, hence of optimal continuation values under any utility and any belief.
∎

## 1.4 The three load-bearing clauses

None may be dropped, and each corresponds to a way the parent statement fails.

1. **The conclusion is about the achievable set and the optimum, not about a
   fixed continuation policy.** This is (D2). Equality for each *fixed*
   continuation is recoverable only by restricting to interface-measurable
   (memoryless-after-the-boundary) policies, which is a strictly smaller class
   than the perfect-recall class of treatment \(H\); a design using that form
   must declare the restriction, and its "optimal value" is then the optimum over
   the restricted class, not \(Q^H\).
2. **(H3) is a hypothesis about the policy class, not a consequence of (H2).**
   It holds automatically when \(\Theta\) is induced by a relabeling of the game
   itself (§1.6), and must be proved otherwise.
3. **The intertwining is checked against the still-leadable structure only.**
   Contexts that can never be led again are dynamically inert and their relations
   may be erased before the check; checking against inert structure costs recall,
   never soundness.

## 1.5 The outcome monoid: a necessary condition, correctly located

Let \(m_a,m_b\) be the increments emitted by the two blocks. (H2) forces the
joint law of the accumulated outcome to agree between the orders, so it forces
\(m_am_b=m_bm_a\) wherever both orders have positive probability: **commutativity
of the emitted increments is a necessary condition for (H2) to be satisfiable**,
not an extra hypothesis of the proof.

Both declared contracts are commutative — \(\mathbb N\cdot e_\star\) for
count-free tricks, \(\mathbb N^2\) for trick-and-score — so commutativity is not
where the count boundary bites. The count obstruction is different and sharper:
in the decorated kernel the emitted increment includes the point value of the
tile focal played, so if the two blocks play tiles of different point values the
*decorated* intertwining (H2) fails even when its count-free shadow holds. This
is the exact mechanism behind the standing rule that no count-free exchangeability
result survives score re-entry without its own proof.

## 1.6 Relation to Proposition J-1, and the count corollary

Proposition J-1 (adjudicated, `walt/CENSUS-RULINGS.md`) supplies the canonical
instance in which \(\Theta\) exists and is exhibited concretely. There the two
blocks are "play tile \(t_1\)" and "play tile \(t_2\)" from the focal hand, and
\(\Theta\) is induced by the transposition \(\tau=(t_1\,t_2)\) extended by the
identity on every other live and table tile, under the hypothesis that \(\tau\)
preserves trump membership, follow membership in every still-leadable context,
the winner-determining order there, the double flag as it enters that order, and
— unless focal provably never leads — the led-context map on \(t_1,t_2\).

**Corollary E1.1.** In the J-1 instance, (H1)–(H3) hold. *Proof.* \(\tau\) is an
automorphism of the remaining extensive game fixing every non-focal holding, so
it carries legal plays to legal plays, field masses to field masses (legal-set
cardinalities are preserved) and increments to increments; the induced map on
traces is the required bijection and satisfies (H2). It carries lawful policies
to lawful policies, giving (H3). ∎

**Corollary E1.2 (count survival under the guard).** If in addition the focal
hand contains no count tile, then (H2) holds in the count-decorated monoid
\(\mathbb N^2\), and the conclusion of Theorem E1 holds for the trick-plus-count
contract. *Proof.* The decorated increment adds the point value of each played
tile. \(\tau\) moves only \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) and fixes every other tile, so the
decoration is preserved exactly when \(c(t_1)=c(t_2)\); the guard gives
\(c(t_1)=c(t_2)=0\). ∎

Theorem E1 is strictly more general than Corollary E1.1: it requires only the
two-block kernel identity up to \(\Theta\), not a full automorphism of the
remaining game. That generality is the content of "promoting D1 to a
trace-equivalence layer", and it is available only with (H1)–(H3) discharged.

# 2. The advantage dimension

*Repairs parent §5; authorised by DS-A3, DS-A4.*

## 2.1 Definition E2 (binding form)

For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Q^{X}\),

\[
\boxed{
d_{\mathrm{adv}}(E)
=
\dim\operatorname{span}
\{\alpha-\alpha':\alpha,\alpha'\in E\},
}
\]

the dimension of the direction space of the affine hull of \(E\).

## 2.2 Why the reference-based form is broken

The parent defines \(d(E;\alpha_0)=\dim\operatorname{span}\{\alpha-\alpha_0:
\alpha\in E\}\) for a reference \(\alpha_0\) "chosen globally across the
candidate set under comparison" — hence possibly outside \(E\) when \(E\) is one
action's envelope.

**Proposition E2.1.** \(d(E;\alpha_0)=d_{\mathrm{adv}}(E)\) if
\(\alpha_0\in\operatorname{aff}(E)\), and \(d(E;\alpha_0)=d_{\mathrm{adv}}(E)+1\)
otherwise.

*Proof.* Fix \(\alpha_1\in E\) and let \(V=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha-\alpha':
\alpha,\alpha'\in E\}\). For each \(\alpha\in E\),
\(\alpha-\alpha_0=(\alpha-\alpha_1)+(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)\), so
\(\operatorname{span}\{\alpha-\alpha_0\}=V+\mathbb Q(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)\). This
equals \(V\) iff \(\alpha_1-\alpha_0\in V\), i.e. iff
\(\alpha_0\in\alpha_1+V=\operatorname{aff}(E)\); otherwise the dimension is one
larger. ∎

**Consequence.** The parent's claim "a singleton envelope has
\(d_{\mathrm{adv}}=0\)" holds under the reference form only when the reference is
that singleton, and fails by exactly one otherwise — which is precisely the
cross-action comparison the parent recommends. Under Definition E2 the claim is
unconditional: \(d_{\mathrm{adv}}(E)=0\iff|E|=1\).

**Properties.** \(d_{\mathrm{adv}}\) is invariant under the common translation
\(\alpha\mapsto\alpha+g\) that parent §5 uses, is monotone in \(E\), and is
bounded by \(\min(|E|-1,|X|)\). The baseline value \(\langle\beta,\alpha_0\rangle\)
must still be retained for absolute \(V\) and \(Q\), exactly as parent §5 says;
the repair concerns only the dimension.

# 3. The action-conditioned upper witness

*Repairs the informal treatment in parent §8.2 and §15.4; authorised by DS-A7.*

## 3.1 The object

For a root action \(a\), define the **action-conditioned world-informed value**

\[
V^{\ast}_a(\xi)
=
\max\Bigl\{
\mathbb E[U\mid\xi,\ a\text{ then }c,\ \sigma_{-m}]
:\ c\ \text{a world-dependent continuation behaviour}
\Bigr\},
\]

the value in world \(\xi\) when the focal seat plays \(a\) at the root and
thereafter chooses with full knowledge of \(\xi\), against the same fixed field.
This is treatment \(C\) with the root action held fixed.

*[Naming clause added 2026-08-13 under DS-A20.]* When the latent is augmented,
\(\xi=(\omega,z)\), **treatment \(C\) reveals the physical world \(\omega\)
only**. Revealing the whole augmented latent — including a persistent field
state, policy type or random tape \(z\) — is a strictly larger relaxation; it is
still a valid upper witness, by the same proof, but it is written \(C^{+}\) and
never called treatment \(C\) unqualified. On the measured carrier
\(\xi=\omega\) and the two coincide.

Set

\[
\boxed{
U_a
=
\mathbb E_\beta\bigl[V^{\ast}_a\bigr]
=
\sum_{\xi}\beta(\xi)V^{\ast}_a(\xi).
}
\]

## 3.2 Lemma E3 (validity of the action-conditioned upper witness)

For every information-consistent \(\rho\in\mathcal R_H(B,a)\) and every
\(\xi\in X_B\),

\[
\alpha_\rho(\xi)\le V^{\ast}_a(\xi),
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
Q^H_B(a)
=
\max_{\rho\in\mathcal R_H(B,a)}\langle\beta,\alpha_\rho\rangle
\;\le\;
U_a .
}
\]

*Proof.* Fix \(\rho\) and \(\xi\). Restricted to world \(\xi\), \(\rho\) induces
a particular world-dependent continuation behaviour — the one it produces along
the plays that occur in \(\xi\) — which is one of the behaviours over which
\(V^{\ast}_a\) maximises. Its expected utility under the field is
\(\alpha_\rho(\xi)\), whence \(\alpha_\rho(\xi)\le V^{\ast}_a(\xi)\). This is the
pointwise strategy-fusion inequality. Multiplying by \(\beta(\xi)\ge0\) and
summing gives \(\langle\beta,\alpha_\rho\rangle\le U_a\) for each \(\rho\); take
the maximum over \(\rho\). ∎

## 3.3 Remark E3.1 — the unconditioned aggregate is valid but action-constant

Let \(V^{\ast}(\xi)=\max_a V^{\ast}_a(\xi)\) be the unconditioned world-informed
value, and \(U^{\mathrm{agg}}=\mathbb E_\beta[V^\ast]\). Then for every \(a\),

\[
Q^H_B(a)\le U_a\le U^{\mathrm{agg}},
\qquad
V^H_B\le\max_a U_a\le U^{\mathrm{agg}} .
\]

So \(U^{\mathrm{agg}}\) is a valid upper witness for every action — using it is
not unsound — but it does not depend on \(a\), so the separation test
\(L_{a^\star}\ge U_a\ (\forall a\ne a^\star)\) degenerates into
\(L_{a^\star}\ge U^{\mathrm{agg}}\), which demands that a single lawful policy
match the full strategy-fusion bound. It is therefore sound and, in general,
vacuous. The branch's existing measurement of \(U^{\mathrm{agg}}-V^H\) is a
root-level quantity and is **not** a per-action information price; the
action-conditioned evaluator must be built, not substituted for.

## 3.4 The four attached conditions

\(U_a\) is an upper witness only when, jointly with the primal witness it is
compared against:

- **(C1)** the same fixed field is used on both sides;
- **(C2)** the same belief and the *same world set* are used on both sides — in
  particular **no decimation may appear inside \(L\) or \(U\)**: a sampled mean
  is neither a lower nor an upper bound, and a separation built on one is void
  (decimation remains lawful for choosing *which* coordinates to certify);
- **(C3)** the same utility and the same count contract are used on both sides;
- **(C4)** perfect-information minimax is not a substitute: it is a different
  operator and is not a valid upper bound for a fixed stochastic field.

# 4. The primal witness, and the way it inverts

*Repairs the informal treatment in parent §8.1 and §15.3; authorised by DS-A14.*

## 4.1 Lemma E4 (validity of a primal witness)

Let \(\rho\in\mathcal R_H(B,a)\) be information-consistent, and let its
world-value vector be computed by the **fixed-policy evaluator**

\[
\alpha_\rho(\xi)=\mathbb E[U\mid\xi,\rho,\sigma_{-m}],
\]

which contains no maximisation at any node below the root. Then for any finite
candidate set \(\mathcal C_a\subseteq\mathcal R_H(B,a)\),

\[
\boxed{
L_a=\max_{\rho\in\mathcal C_a}\langle\beta,\alpha_\rho\rangle
\;\le\;
Q^H_B(a),
}
\]

with equality iff some candidate attains the maximum over
\(\mathcal R_H(B,a)\).

*Proof.* \(\mathcal C_a\subseteq\mathcal R_H(B,a)\) and \(Q^H_B(a)\) is the
maximum of the same objective over the larger set. ∎

The proof is one line; the content is entirely in the two hypotheses —
*information-consistency* and *fixed-policy evaluation*. Both are load-bearing,
and the second fails silently.

## 4.2 Non-theorem E4′ (the inversion failure mode)

Suppose \(\widehat L=\langle\beta,\widehat\alpha\rangle\) is computed with any
evaluator that maximises inside the world after conditioning on \(\xi\) — a
world-informed rollout, treatment \(C\) or \(F\), a revealed-world solver, an
unconditioned world-informed aggregate, or a per-world best response. By Lemma E3
\(\widehat\alpha\ge\alpha_\rho\) pointwise for every information-consistent
\(\rho\), so \(\widehat L\) is an **upper** bound and the inequality
\(\widehat L\le Q^H_B(a)\) may fail. Installing \(\widehat L\) as \(L\) in the
separation test breaks the chain

\[
Q^H_B(a^\star)\;\ge\;L_{a^\star}\;\ge\;U_a\;\ge\;Q^H_B(a)
\]

at its first inequality, and the test can certify an action that is not optimal
while every displayed step still typechecks.

**A minimal witness that the failure is real.** Two worlds
\(\xi_1,\xi_2\) with \(\beta\) uniform; after root action \(a^\star\) the focal
seat cannot distinguish them and has two continuations \(c_1,c_2\) with world
values \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) respectively. Every information-consistent policy
must choose one continuation at the single information state, so
\(Q^H(a^\star)=\tfrac12\). The world-informed price is
\(\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac12\cdot1=1\). If a competitor \(a\) has
\(U_a=\tfrac34\ge Q^H(a)\), then installing \(\widehat L_{a^\star}=1\) certifies
\(a^\star\) by \(1\ge\tfrac34\), while in truth
\(Q^H(a^\star)=\tfrac12<Q^H(a)\) is possible. The certified action can be
strictly worse than the rejected one.

**Obligation.** *[Restated semantically 2026-08-13 under DS-A27.]* The binding
invariant is semantic:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{every later focal action is supplied by the candidate policy, and}\\
\text{no optimiser selects a focal action using hidden-state information.}
\end{array}
}
\]

Nonfocal expectation, chance summation, deterministic singleton choices and
implementation maxima over singleton sets do not violate it. "No max node below
the root" remains an accepted **sufficient** implementation form — cheap to
assert structurally and impossible to game — but it is a receipt for the
invariant, not the invariant. Either way the assertion is structural, not by
inspection, and every run asserts \(L\le Q^H\) at every coordinate where
treatment \(H\) completes.

# 5. The Scheme-mass closure is degenerate in this game

*Repairs the reading of parent §10.3; authorised by DS-A5.*

## 5.1 Proposition E5

*[Statement amended 2026-08-13 under DS-A20: hypothesis (S) added explicitly.
The conclusion and the proof are unchanged on every carrier the branch has
measured; see Lemma E5.0.]*

Consider the parent's §10.3 hypotheses imposed at **every reachable interface**
of the finite-horizon fixed-field process: (1) \(1\in\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_t)\);
(2) terminal readouts lie in the terminal algebra; (3) for every successor atom
\(A'\), \(\operatorname{Pre}_{a,o}(\mathbf 1_{A'})\in\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_t)\);
(4) immediate outcome functions lie in \(\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_t)\). Assume a
field of full support on legal plays, and

- **(S) latent separation by complete records.** For all
  \(\xi\ne\xi'\in X_{\mathfrak i}\) there is a complete record \(r\) with
  \(\Pr_\xi(r)\ne\Pr_{\xi'}(r)\).

Then at every interface \(\mathfrak i\) the algebra \(\mathfrak A_{\mathfrak i}\)
separates the points of \(X_{\mathfrak i}\): its atoms are singletons and

\[
\boxed{
\dim\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_{\mathfrak i})=|X_{\mathfrak i}| .
}
\]

*Proof.* By (1) the constant \(1\) lies in the terminal algebra's span. By (3),
applied backwards along a single observation path \(o_1,\dots,o_k\) with any
legal controllers, the composite

\[
\operatorname{Pre}_{a_1,o_1}\bigl(\cdots\operatorname{Pre}_{a_k,o_k}(1)\cdots\bigr)
\]

lies in \(\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_{\mathfrak i})\); as a function of \(\xi\) it
equals \(\Pr_\xi(o_1\cdots o_k\mid\text{controllers})\). Take \(k\) to the
horizon, so the record is complete. Hence \(\mathrm{SF}(\mathfrak A_{\mathfrak i})\)
contains the function \(\xi\mapsto\Pr_\xi(r)\) for every complete record \(r\),
and therefore separates any two latents that (S) separates — that is, all of
them. A finite Boolean algebra whose span separates points has singleton atoms,
and \(\mathrm{SF}\) has dimension equal to the number of atoms. ∎

## 5.1a Lemma E5.0 — (S) holds on the measured carrier

*Added 2026-08-13 under DS-A20.*

Suppose the latent state is exactly the hidden current remainder — a world is an
assignment of the unplayed tiles to the three hidden seats, with no persistent
field state, policy type or random tape — and the field is the memoryless
uniform-legal profile. Then (S) holds, and moreover
\(\Pr_\cdot(r)=w_r\cdot\mathbf 1[\,\cdot=\xi_r]\) with \(w_r>0\) for each
complete record \(r\).

*Proof.* Given \(\xi\) consistent with \(r\), the field's masses along \(r\) are
products of unit fractions, so \(\Pr_\xi(r)>0\); given \(\xi\) inconsistent with
\(r\), some play in \(r\) is unavailable and \(\Pr_\xi(r)=0\). A complete record
names every hidden tile together with the seat that played it, so at most one
world is consistent with it, and every world is consistent with some complete
record because any world can be played out under a field of full support. ∎

**This is the carrier of every measurement the branch has taken** — the
void-free capacity fiber under the §7.4 uniform-legal field. Proposition E5's
conclusion therefore stands unconditionally for S6a, S6b and S6c. Hypothesis (S)
is required only if the latent is later augmented, \(\xi=(\omega,z)\), with a
component \(z\) that no complete record resolves; in that case the argument
yields indicators of complete-record equivalence classes rather than singletons,
which is a weaker and possibly compressive conclusion.

## 5.2 Corollaries

**E5.1 (no compression claim).** No compression claim may be read from parent
§10.3 in this game. A reported atom count below \(|X|\) means hypothesis (3) was
not imposed at every reachable interface — the algebra is then purpose-relative
and must be declared as such, with the purpose named.

**E5.2 (what survives, and what forces the degeneracy).** *[Language narrowed
2026-08-13 under DS-A21.]* The fixed-policy evaluation claim survives in the
purpose-relative form: seed the closure with the declared contract's readouts —
the zero space for the count-free expected-trick contract — and close under
**observation-aggregated** residuals of those readouts only. That is the value
closure \(\mathcal V^{\mathrm{val}}\) of Lemma R, which never admits the constant
and therefore escapes the argument above. What forces the degeneracy is exactly
the constant: exact **normalised** filtering needs \(\operatorname{Pre}(1)\) at
every step. The binding statement of the negative is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{atom-mass \emph{linear} filtering is noncompressive on this carrier,}
}
\]

and **not** "filtering and compression are incompatible" unqualified. Proposition
E5 rules out a small universal atom algebra closed under complete-record
residuals. It does not rule out a compact arithmetic or Boolean circuit, a
factorised representation, a BDD/ZDD-style exact form, fixed-prior symbolic
propagation, purpose-specific moment compilation, or fixed-policy weighted model
counting — any of which may represent the same posterior implicitly without
materialising \(|X|\) atom masses. The unnormalised value recursion is likewise
untouched.

## 5.3 The reframe this forces

*[Sharpened 2026-08-13 under DS-A21.]*

Matrix rank remains a lower bound on linear factorisations, and Proposition E5
says the relevant algebra saturates. What changes is not the bound but the
**target**. A solver that certifies an action never needs to represent every
value function on the fiber; it needs to evaluate finitely many inner products
\(\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\) for finitely many lawful \(\alpha\), together with
valid bounds. The precise statement of what the measured rank does and does not
bound: **predictive rank lower-bounds the corresponding linear factorisation
target; it does not by itself lower-bound the size of an unrestricted nonlinear
arithmetic or Boolean DAG.** Nothing here asserts that no lower-bound technique
applies to circuits — only that no result in this branch supplies one.

This is a legitimate change of target and, in this adjudicator's view, the
parent document's best idea — but it must be stated as a change of target and
never as circuits beating a rank bound. The two are different claims and only the
first is available.

## Theorem E6.1 — width monotonicity (parent §4.3; audit T2)

Let \(\mathcal B'\subseteq\mathcal B\subseteq\Delta(X_B)\) be belief families
**typed on a common world space**: a world is a full hidden deal, unchanged by
play, and an observation refines the belief on that same space. Then
\(W_{\mathcal B'}(B,a)\le W_{\mathcal B}(B,a)\); in particular
\(W_{\mathrm{reach}}\le W_{\mathrm{all}}\).

*Proof.* Any envelope-sufficient set on the larger family is envelope-sufficient
on the subfamily; take minima. ∎

**The typing clause is not cosmetic.** Posteriors naturally live at successor
interfaces over smaller fibers; the comparison with \(\alpha\)-vectors on \(X_B\)
is well typed only in the deal-level view. Any computation of
\(W_{\mathrm{reach}}\) declares the identification between the two views and
holds to it. Note also that on a proper subfamily the minimising envelope need
not be unique, so \(W_{\mathrm{reach}}\) is a well-defined number but "the
reachable envelope" is not a well-defined set, and nothing may be keyed to it.

*[Superseded in part 2026-08-13 under DS-A23: the deal-level identification makes
the root-level quantity well typed but does not make it the operationally
meaningful one. The primary object is the interface-local
\(W^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(I,a)\) of Definition E9 (§8.4); the
root-level \(W_{\mathrm{reach}}(B,a)\) remains well defined and is a different
quantity answering a different question.]*

Finally, at the full simplex the minimal envelope **is** unique and equals the
set of vectors that are uniquely optimal for some belief (Lemma G(4)); hence
\(W_{\mathrm{all}}=N_{\exp}\) — one object under two names, never two rows in a
results file.

## Theorem E6.2 — backward pruning (parent §6.3, completed; audit T5)

Parent §6.3 proves monotonicity: if \(P\) is a nonnegative (sub)stochastic
operator and \(f\le g\) pointwise then \(Pf\le Pg\) pointwise. That is necessary
but not sufficient for exact backward pruning. The two additional facts, restated
here with proof so this document is self-contained (they are Lemma G(2), G(3)):

**(a) Frontier reproduction.** Let the composition at an interface be
\(C(v_1,\dots,v_k)=g+\sum_{i}T_i(v_i)\) with each \(T_i\) a positive linear
operator and one independent successor choice per player observation. Let \(R\)
be the set of composed vectors built from the full successor sets \(S_i\), and
\(R'\) the set built from the Pareto-maximal subsets \(\max(S_i)\). Then
\(\max(R)\subseteq R'\) and \(\max(R')=\max(R)\).

*Proof.* Let \(C(v)\in\max(R)\). For each \(i\) choose \(w_i\in\max(S_i)\) with
\(v_i\le w_i\) (a finite poset has a maximal element above every element). By
monotonicity \(C(v)\le C(w)\in R\); maximality of \(C(v)\) forces
\(C(v)=C(w)\in R'\). Hence \(\max(R)\subseteq R'\), and its elements are maximal
in \(R'\subseteq R\) a fortiori. Conversely if \(x\in\max(R')\) were not maximal
in \(R\), some \(y\in R\) has \(y>x\); choosing \(y'\in\max(R)\) with \(y\le y'\)
gives \(y'\in R'\) and \(y'>x\), contradiction. ∎

**(b) Incremental pruning is exact, and is required.** With \(\oplus\) the
elementwise sum of two vector sets,
\(\max\bigl((\max(A\oplus B))\oplus C\bigr)=\max(A\oplus B\oplus C)\), by the
same argument. Without it the fold materialises \(\prod_i|S_i|\) combinations
before any pruning can occur, so per-interface pruning alone does not make the
computation feasible.

**(c) What pruning destroys.** *[Narrowed 2026-08-13 under DS-A26.]* Pareto
pruning and convex-dominance pruning do not preserve the count of distinct policy
vectors in general — each discards vectors that the count counts. Binding rule: a
pruned run may not report that count unless it maintains a separate complete
unpruned accounting of it, which the pruning was adopted to avoid. (The
degenerate rules that discard only duplicates preserve it trivially; the claim is
about the two rules actually in use.)

## Theorem E6.3 — value sandwich (parent §8.3; audit T7)

Let \(\mathcal C_a\subseteq\mathcal R_H(B,a)\subseteq\mathcal R^+_a\), where all
three sets are evaluated with the **same \(\alpha\)-map** — same field, same
mechanics, same utility, same count contract — and all three maxima are taken at
the **same belief** \(\beta\) over the **same world set**, with no decimation
anywhere inside either bound (condition (C2) of §3.4). Then

\[
L_a(\beta)\le Q^H_B(a)\le U_a(\beta),
\]

and \(L_a(\beta)=U_a(\beta)\) forces both to equal \(Q^H_B(a)\).

*Proof.* Nested sets, one objective; equal endpoints trap the middle. ∎

## Theorem E6.4 — root-action separation (parent §8.4; audit T8)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem E6.3 for every action, if
\(L_{a^\star}(\beta)\ge U_a(\beta)\) for every \(a\ne a^\star\), then
\(a^\star\in\operatorname{Opt}^H(B)\); if every inequality is strict then
\(\operatorname{Opt}^H(B)=\{a^\star\}\).

*Proof.* \(Q^H(a^\star)\ge L_{a^\star}\ge U_a\ge Q^H(a)\) for each competitor. ∎

**Caveat in the statement, not the commentary:** the non-strict form certifies a
*member* of the optimal set, not the set. Where the full optimal set is wanted
the separation is run per action, or the report says "contains \(a^\star\)".

## Theorem E6.5 — finite adaptive gluing (parent §9.2; audit T9)

Let \(\mathcal R_0(a)\supseteq\mathcal R_H(B,a)\) be a declared relaxation and
let each iteration add an information equality satisfied by every \(H\)-policy
and violated by at least one member of \(\mathcal R_k(a)\). Assume in addition:

- **(G1)** each iteration's relaxed solve returns the exact maximum over
  \(\mathcal R_k(a)\), or at minimum a *proved* upper bound on it — proved per
  solver, not assumed; otherwise \(U^{(k)}_a\) is not a bound and the sandwich
  fails;
- **(G2)** the stopping test is the exposed-face criterion
  \(\operatorname{Opt}(\mathcal R_k,\beta)\cap\mathcal R_H\ne\varnothing\), which
  is a search over the whole optimal face: a negative answer requires proof,
  whereas an unlawful returned optimiser licenses only the addition of a cut.

Then \(U^{(0)}_a\ge U^{(1)}_a\ge\cdots\ge Q^H_B(a)\); if the criterion of (G2)
holds at iteration \(k\) then \(U^{(k)}_a=Q^H_B(a)\); and the procedure
terminates.

*Proof.* Monotonicity and validity are set inclusion. If an \(H\)-consistent
policy attains the relaxed maximum it lies in both sets, so the maxima coincide.
Each iteration removes at least one member of a finite set, so the process
terminates. ∎

**The termination bound is \(|\mathcal R_0(a)|\)** — doubly exponential — and
must never be read as a complexity claim. Whether the number of *active* cuts is
small is the experimental question, not a corollary.

A cut constrains the **relaxation**, never the lawful policy class and never the
fiber. A "cut" applied to worlds is a declared exclusion remnant — analyst
conditioning on a non-evidential predicate — and carries none of the guarantees
above.

## 8.1 Theorem E1′ — order exchange under a fully transported involution

*Generalises Theorem E1 (§1.3); authorised by DS-A19.*

Let the two-block kernels be as in §1.2. Suppose there are bijections

- \(\Theta_T\) on traces, carrying \(T_{ab}\) onto \(T_{ba}\);
- \(\Theta_M\) on the outcome monoid \(\mathcal M\), a monoid automorphism,
  often the identity;
- \(\Theta_X\) on successor latent states, carrying information interfaces to
  information interfaces;

such that:

- **(H1′)** \(\Theta=(\Theta_T,\Theta_M,\Theta_X)\) is declared, with its
  inverse, before any check;
- **(H2′) intertwining.** \(\mathsf K_{ba}=\Theta_*\mathsf K_{ab}\), i.e. for
  every \(\xi,\tau,m,\xi'\),
  \(\mathsf K_{ba}(\xi;\Theta_T\tau,\Theta_M m,\Theta_X\xi')
  =\mathsf K_{ab}(\xi;\tau,m,\xi')\);
- **(H3′) policy-class closure.** For every lawful continuation \(\pi\) from the
  \(ab\)-successors there is a lawful \(\pi^\Theta\) from the
  \(ba\)-successors with \(\pi^\Theta(\Theta_T\tau)\) the \(\Theta\)-image of
  \(\pi(\tau)\), and conversely;
- **(H4′) continuation equivariance.**
  \(\Lambda_{\pi^\Theta(\Theta_T\tau)}(\Theta_X\xi')
  =(\Theta_M)_*\Lambda_{\pi(\tau)}(\xi')\) for every lawful \(\pi\), trace
  \(\tau\) and successor \(\xi'\);
- **(H5′) utility invariance.** The selected utility satisfies
  \(U\circ\Theta_M=U\).

Then for every \(\xi\) the sets of achievable terminal laws under the two orders
agree up to \(\Theta_M\), and under (H5′) the two orders have the same optimal
continuation value for every belief.

*Proof.* Fix \(\xi\) and a lawful \(\pi\). As in §1.3,

\[
\mathcal L(ab,\pi)(\xi)
=\sum_{\tau,m,\xi'}\mathsf K_{ab}(\xi;\tau,m,\xi')\;
m\cdot\Lambda_{\pi(\tau)}(\xi').
\]

Reindex the \((ba)\) sum by \((\Theta_T\tau,\Theta_M m,\Theta_X\xi')\) and apply
(H2′) and then (H4′):

\[
\mathcal L(ba,\pi^\Theta)(\xi)
=\sum_{\tau,m,\xi'}\mathsf K_{ab}(\xi;\tau,m,\xi')\;
\Theta_M(m)\cdot(\Theta_M)_*\Lambda_{\pi(\tau)}(\xi')
=(\Theta_M)_*\mathcal L(ab,\pi)(\xi),
\]

using that \(\Theta_M\) is a monoid automorphism to pull it out of the
translation. By (H3′) the correspondence \(\pi\mapsto\pi^\Theta\) is a bijection
of the lawful classes, so the achievable sets correspond under
\((\Theta_M)_*\). Under (H5′) the utility of a law equals the utility of its
\(\Theta_M\)-image, so the achievable *values* coincide as sets and hence have
equal maxima, for every belief. ∎

**Specialisation.** Theorem E1 is the case \(\Theta_M=\mathrm{id}\),
\(\Theta_X=\mathrm{id}\), where (H4′) is automatic because \(\Lambda\) is
literally the same function on both sides and (H5′) is vacuous.

**Remark (the two new hypotheses are not decoration).** (H4′) is what fails if
one transports successor states without transporting the process that runs from
them; the automorphism instance of Corollary E1.1 supplies it for free, and any
other instance must prove it. (H5′) is the same condition as the valuation
restriction of Lemma E8 below, seen from the kernel side: for the count-free
contract \(\Theta_M=\mathrm{id}\) and it is vacuous; for the count-decorated
monoid it demands that the exchanged tiles carry equal point values, which is
exactly what the no-count guard supplies.

## 8.2 Corollary E3.2 — the zero-global-gap corollary

*Extends Lemma E3 (§3.2); authorised by DS-A22.*

Suppose \(U^{\mathrm{agg}}=V^H_B\). Then for **every** \(H\)-optimal root action
\(a^\star\),

\[
\boxed{
U_{a^\star}=Q^H_B(a^\star)=V^H_B ,
}
\]

and moreover \(U_a\le V^H_B\) for every root action \(a\).

*Proof.* \(V^H=Q^H(a^\star)\le U_{a^\star}\le U^{\mathrm{agg}}=V^H\) by Lemma E3
and Remark E3.1, so all four are equal. For arbitrary \(a\),
\(U_a\le U^{\mathrm{agg}}=V^H\). ∎

**Consequence (the useful one).** If in addition a primal witness attains the
optimum, \(L_{a^\star}=V^H\), then \(L_{a^\star}\ge U_a\) for every \(a\), so
Theorem E6.4 certifies \(a^\star\in\operatorname{Opt}^H(B)\) with no gluing
iteration at all. At a coordinate with zero measured global fusion gap, the
entire remaining difficulty is on the **primal** side.

**Three non-implications, and they matter.** The corollary does **not** imply
(i) that \(U_a\) is tight for a suboptimal \(a\) — it bounds \(U_a\) by \(V^H\)
and says nothing about \(Q^H(a)\); (ii) that the separation is strict — every
inequality it supplies is non-strict, so it certifies membership in the optimal
set and never uniqueness (Theorem E6.4's member-not-set caveat); (iii) that a
primal witness attaining \(V^H\) has been found — the corollary supplies no
witness, and finding one is the whole primal problem.

## 8.3 Lemma E7 — when dominance travels

*Authorised by DS-A25; narrows the "dominance never travels" rule.*

Let \(T:X\to X'\) be a bijection of latent domains and \(\rho\mapsto T\rho\) a
bijection of the lawful policy classes \(\mathcal R_H(I,a)\to\mathcal R_H(I',a')\)
such that

\[
\boxed{\alpha_{T\rho}(T\xi)=\alpha_\rho(\xi)
\qquad\forall\rho,\ \forall\xi.}
\]

Then:

1. \(\alpha_\rho\preceq\alpha_\sigma\iff\alpha_{T\rho}\preceq\alpha_{T\sigma}\);
   hence Pareto frontiers correspond, the frontier count transports, universal
   dominance transports, and decision-deadness transports;
2. for every belief \(\beta\) on \(X\) and its pushforward
   \(\beta'=T_*\beta\), \(\langle\beta',\alpha_{T\rho}\rangle
   =\langle\beta,\alpha_\rho\rangle\); hence optimality at a belief, exposure,
   and decision width transport **provided the belief is transported too**.

*Proof.* (1) The two families of scalar inequalities are the same set
re-indexed by \(T\). (2)
\(\sum_{\xi'}\beta'(\xi')\alpha_{T\rho}(\xi')
=\sum_{\xi}\beta(\xi)\alpha_{T\rho}(T\xi)=\sum_\xi\beta(\xi)\alpha_\rho(\xi)\).
∎

**Binding form of the library rule.** *Dominance does not travel with a policy
alone.* A policy transport establishes only that the transported object is a
lawful policy at the new coordinate — which is what makes it a valid primal
witness, and is all that is needed for that. Transporting a *verdict* —
dominance, optimality, deadness, a width — additionally requires an exhibited
value-order isomorphism satisfying the displayed identity, together with
transport of the belief for the belief-relative verdicts. Corollary R-fold is an
instance of exactly this and may be cited as one; nothing else in the branch
currently is.

## 8.4 Definition E9 — interface-local reachable decision width

*Retypes the reachable width of §6.1 (Theorem E6.1); authorised by DS-A23.*

For an information interface \(I\), let \(X_I\) be its latent domain,
\(\mathcal A_{I,a}\) the set of value vectors of lawful continuation policies
**beginning at \(I\)** with first action \(a\), and
\(\mathcal B_{\mathrm{reach}}(I)\subseteq\Delta(X_I)\) the set of posteriors with
which \(I\) is actually reached, under the declared initial belief, the fixed
field, legal focal policies and positive-probability observation histories. Then

\[
\boxed{
W^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(I,a)
=\min\Bigl\{|E|:E\subseteq\mathcal A_{I,a},\
\max_{\alpha\in E}\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle=Q_I(a;\beta)
\ \ \forall\beta\in\mathcal B_{\mathrm{reach}}(I)\Bigr\}.
}
\]

This is the quantity that answers "how many policies must the seat retain here",
because a posterior generated by a prefix evaluates continuations that begin
where that prefix ends. Theorem E6.1's monotonicity holds interface-wise:
\(W^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(I,a)\le W_{\mathrm{all}}(I,a)\), and
\(W_{\mathrm{all}}(I,a)=N_{\exp}(I,a)\) as before.

**Three quantities, three names, never one row.** (i) the interface-local width
above; (ii) a global summary such as \(\max_I W^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(I,a)\);
(iii) the size of a single transported policy library covering all reachable
interfaces — which is smaller than a sum and larger than a max in general, and
is a library statistic, not a width. The root-level \(W_{\mathrm{reach}}(B,a)\)
of §6.1 remains well defined in the deal-level view and is a fourth quantity: it
asks how many *root* policy vectors preserve the root envelope over a family of
later posteriors, which is a question no seat asks.

## 8.5 Lemma E8 — the exact valuation scope of J-0 and J-1

*Corrects the cone clause of ruling DS-A9; authorised by DS-A24. The mathematics
of Lemma J and Propositions J-0, J-1 is unchanged.*

Work in the parent's feature coordinates: \(\phi_T=(t_T,(x_{T,d})_{d\in\mathcal D})\)
with \(x_{T,d}=1\) iff the focal partnership captures tile \(d\), and
\(\mu_\rho(\xi)=\mathbb E[\phi_T\mid\xi,\rho,\sigma_{-m}]\). A valuation is a
pair \(v=(b,w)\) with \(w:\mathcal D\to\mathbb Q\), and
\(\alpha^v_\rho=\langle v,\mu_\rho\rangle\).

**(a) The invariant part.** Assume the hypothesis (NI) of Lemma J at node \(I\)
(in particular, as established by Proposition J-0). Let \(H\) be the focal
seat's remaining hand and let \(\rho,\rho'\) be any two lawful policies. Then for
every \(\xi\):

1. the trick coordinate agrees: \(t_T\) has the same expectation;
2. \(x_{T,d}\) has the same expectation for every \(d\notin H\);
3. \(\sum_{d\in H}x_{T,d}\) has the same expectation.

*Proof.* (1) and (2) are Lemma J(a): the non-focal plays and the trick winners
have one joint law independent of the focal policy, and every tile outside \(H\)
is either already played or played by a non-focal seat, so which trick it falls
in and who wins that trick are policy-independent. (3) The focal partnership
captures exactly four tiles per trick it wins, so
\(\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}x_{T,d}=4t_T\); subtract the policy-independent quantity
\(\sum_{d\notin H}x_{T,d}\) from the policy-independent \(4t_T\). ∎

**(b) The exact condition on the valuation.** Consequently

\[
\alpha^v_\rho(\xi)-\alpha^v_{\rho'}(\xi)
=\sum_{d\in H}w(d)\,\Delta_d,
\qquad
\sum_{d\in H}\Delta_d=0,
\]

which vanishes for all \(\rho,\rho',\xi\) whenever

\[
\boxed{w\ \text{is constant on }H.}
\]

This is sufficient always. It is also necessary whenever some pair of lawful
policies moves one tile of \(H\) into a partnership-won trick and another out of
one with positive probability, which is the generic case.

**(c) What this corrects.** Ruling DS-A9 stated that under J-0 or J-1 with the
guard the feature difference vanishes identically and the conclusion therefore
"holds for every cone at once". That is **false in fixed physical coordinates**:
two zero-count focal tiles exchanged between tricks are indistinguishable to the
ordinary count schedule but not to an arbitrary per-tile valuation with
\(w(t_1)\ne w(t_2)\). The correct scope is (b): the equality extends to the
valuation directions that are constant on the exchanged tiles — for
Proposition J-1's transposition, exactly those with \(w(t_1)=w(t_2)\), i.e.
\(w\circ\Theta=w\) — or to role-indexed valuations transported with the roles.
Everything the branch has actually claimed survives: count-free trick value
(\(w\equiv0\)), and ordinary Straight count under the guard
(\(w|_H\equiv0\)).

**(d) Gauge stability.** The condition is stable under the parent's §11.1 gauge
\((b,w)\sim(b-4c,\ w+c\mathbf 1)\), since adding a global constant to \(w\)
preserves constancy on \(H\). It is therefore a condition on the valuation
class, not on a chosen representative.

**(e) Lemma J(c′) — the corrected and strengthened valuation clause.** Under
(NI), the value is identical for every lawful policy for every valuation that
reads the play through the trick winners and a tile-value schedule \(w\) that is
**constant on \(H\)**; the ordinary count schedule under the guard
\(H\cap\mathrm{COUNT}=\varnothing\) is the case \(w|_H\equiv0\).

*Proof.* The focal seat plays exactly one tile to each remaining trick, so if
\(w|_H\equiv k\) then each remaining trick receives the same tile-value
contribution \(k\) from the focal seat whichever tile it plays; the other three
tiles of each trick and the winner of each trick are policy-independent by
Lemma J(a). ∎

This is strictly stronger than the clause it replaces (constancy, not vanishing)
and its proof is the same argument; the filed Lemma J(c) is the special case
\(k=0\) and is sound as filed. Only DS-A9's gloss overreached.
